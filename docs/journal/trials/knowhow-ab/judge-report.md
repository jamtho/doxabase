# Blind Review: Method Pages — Design 1 vs Design 2
*(Judge: separate Fable instance, blind to conditions; Design 1 = B/capsule, Design 2 = A/baseline — unblinded after judging. ~40 factual claims spot-checked against the live capsule, the shipped workbench package, and the journal record.)*

## Per-criterion scores

### 1. Constraint fidelity — Design 1: 5, Design 2: 4
Design 1 follows every named law and argues each from shipped precedent: failure modes placed before invariants/parameters exactly mirroring dataset.html's caveats-before-columns; read-only discipline explicit; refuses every synthetic trust surface; reuses caveat_card, the anchored-derivation provenance treatment, and _data_root() rather than inventing parallel surfaces — all reuse claims verified real. Design 2 equally disciplined on read-only/evidence/no-scoring, and its header is arguably the best implementation of "lead with what you're trying to show" in either design — but it deliberately moves failure modes to position 6, a self-aware, argued deviation from the loudest recorded law.

### 2. Failure-mode anticipation — Design 1: 5, Design 2: 4
Design 1's spine is a catalog of ways the page could mislead, each verified real, each turned into a rendering rule plus acceptance check: the anomalous M12→M11 dependsOnMethod edge, M13's output frames carrying no edge to the contract, cross-contract parameter reuse with ownership resolution and no-owner fallback, missing plot files, one-time-verification prose that must not become a live-looking pass/fail pill. Design 2 excellent on prose-echo fragility, invariant-comment ambiguity, overloaded patternTarget resolution, dead-end prevention, and the best degradation test in either design — but has one verified breakage: its invariant constrainedBy pills dead-end on M13's page for m12-silence-gap (not among M13's parameters). Design 1 designed exactly this case; Design 2 missed it.

### 3. Specificity — Design 1: 4, Design 2: 5
Design 1 extraordinarily verifiable (contract counts, verbatim quotes, shared-caveat pairs, namespaces, plot filenames, workbench code references all true) — docked for three inaccuracies: the daily-index claim is false for M11 (its contract declares six daily-index columns); "~4,700 characters" overstated (max 4,033); M11's realization mislabelled "never-materialized" (it was exercised at survey scale; the full-population frame is what never materialized). Its contract-only scope also leaves the graph knowing more than the page shows. Design 2's capsule census is deeper and checked out almost perfectly (47 patterns / 13 methods; the exact 10-of-14 citesClaim split with the correct four uncited parameters; feed-outages structure; session-14 pilot numbers verbatim). Its one clean factual error sits in an illustrative mock, contradicted by its own correct §3.2.

### 4. Taste alignment — Design 1: 5, Design 2: 4
Design 1 reads like the record's own voice: caveat-forward, prose-as-the-audited-layer, "indexical, not timeless" provenance reuse, vocabulary gaps flagged as couldn't-say debt rather than patched in the UI, "render the wart, let the human catch it" applied to a real wart it found itself. Design 2 strongly aligned in most respects; the caveats-later call and always-visible inactive comparison affordance are two small departures from the recorded instinct, both flagged but both real.

## Winner: Design 1 (19–17)
This is a trust surface, and Design 1 treats the page's honesty obligations as the design's load-bearing structure: it went looking for the places the graph could mislead a reader, verified each against the real capsule, and made each into a rendering rule plus a runnable acceptance check. Design 2 is the better map of the whole capsule — methods-as-patterns with contract enrichment is arguably the truer reading of the task, it covers the ten uncontracted methods, and it recovered output-dataset edges the winner's approach cannot show — but it deviates from the strongest recorded information-design law, contains a verified design breakage on a real capsule case, and its flagship mock contradicts both the capsule and its own §3.2. Build Design 1; steal Design 2's header decision surface and its pattern-level output-edge resolution.

## Factual errors found
Design 1: (1) FALSE: M11/M12 daily-index "no individual columns" — M11 declares six; true only for M12. (2) Overstated: "~4,700 chars" claim (max 4,033). (3) Misleading: M11 realization "never-materialized." (4) Interpretive overreach: "almost certainly mis-wired" for M12→M11 — the glitch parameters cite M11's survey evidence, so a deliberate knowledge-dependency reading is at least as plausible; render-as-recorded is robust either way. (5) Minor: run-in-heading regex misses M12's actual heading; safe-fallback covers it.
Design 2: (1) FALSE + internal contradiction: header mock's "depends on: (none formal)" for M12 vs its own §3.2 chain diagram. (2) Minor: mock omits M12's daily-index consumption. (3) Trivial: "verbatim on all three realizations" — M13's variant differs. (4) Unverified-as-presented: search-ranking observations. (5) Design gap (verified): constrainedBy pills dead-end on M13's cross-contract case.

## Best idea in each
Design 1: the cross-contract parameter-reuse treatment — detect constrainedBy targets outside the contract's own hasParameter set, reverse-query the owning contract, render "defined in the M12 contract, reused here by reference" with honest no-owner fallback; verified real case, algorithm plus acceptance check — and exactly the case the rival breaks on.
Design 2: the header as a complete trust-triage decision surface — outputMeaning, consumed datasets, an orients-not-grades depth badge, and the failure-mode count above any prose wall, so the analyst can rule a method in or out before paying any reading cost. Directly liftable into Design 1's build.
