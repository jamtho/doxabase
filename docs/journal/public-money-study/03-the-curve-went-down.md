# The Curve Went Down

*Public-money study, article 3. We measured whether a growing capsule
of recorded know-how makes fresh agents better at the domain — with a
sealed benchmark, blind grading, and a result we didn't want but can
defend. Methodology only; the working sessions live privately.*

The premise of this whole program is that recorded, structured
knowledge transfers — that an agent handed a capsule of a project's
accumulated understanding performs like a colleague rather than a
stranger. For the spending-data domain we finally measured it the hard
way. The design: a 13-question benchmark (ten entity-resolution chains
— from a payment row to a defensible identification or a defensible
refusal — plus three coverage/concentration macros), written and
hash-sealed *before* any measurement ran, its questions never entering
the capsule. Then four rounds: after each working session grew the
capsule, a completely fresh agent — same model, same neutral
instructions, no project context — got the capsule, the raw data, and
nothing else, and sat the same exam. An answer key was adjudicated at
the end from the frozen sources only, by an agent who never saw any
round's answers; a separate judge then graded all four rounds in one
blind pass, labels stripped. Every artifact was hashed at freeze; the
grading dimensions, deviation rules, and counterweights were
pre-committed in the seal.

If recorded know-how compounds, the curve should rise. It went
77 → 75 → 73 → 73 out of 78.

**First, the result that held: the honesty floor.** The benchmark's
zero-tolerance rule scores an entire question zero for asserting a
registered identity the frozen evidence doesn't support, and the judge
spot-checked every load-bearing citation against the frozen data —
about sixty checks. Across all four rounds: **zero hallucinated
resolutions, zero fabricated citations.** Four different cold agents
independently refused the same five-way candidate tie, the same
trading-name dead-end, the same payee-less row, the same
common-token blocking gap — and said so with evidence, which the key
scores as full credit, because in this domain "this cannot be resolved
from these sources" is often the correct professional answer. We saw
the same floor hold in our previous domain's sealed trial. Whatever
else the capsule discipline does, it makes *not lying* transfer.

**Then the result that stung.** The sharpest per-question trajectory
declined monotonically: 6, 4, 4, 3. The anatomy is worth every word.
One supplier's automated match to the company register rested — as we
only established at adjudication — on a *stale previous name*: the
matched company had renamed years earlier, and the sources point to a
sibling entity in the same corporate family as the actual counterparty.
The baseline agent, with almost no recorded apparatus to lean on, read
the primary registry snapshot carefully and gave the answer the key
sanctions: both candidates, explicitly declining to pick. The later
agents had more: a derived matching table carrying the stale match with
an "exact" grade, and a recorded method — "a register entry naming the
supplier corroborates the identification, promoting its confidence
tier" — that had genuinely worked in earlier sessions. They applied
the method to a bare-name register row the key assigns to the *other*
entity, and confidently promoted the wrong company. The recorded
apparatus didn't just fail to help; **it transmitted a subtle error at
promotion-grade confidence, and a recorded method was applied by cold
consumers without the scope judgement its originator had.**

The rest of the decline has the same texture, not the same cause: a
grain trap in a derived table's own key structure caught two rounds'
concentration figures; the round with the most recorded caveats
available was the only one to drop a mandatory caveat from a headline
number. Meanwhile the questions whose answers HAD been correctly
recorded ran near-perfect in every round — later agents found the
recorded claims, cited them, and (to their credit) cross-checked
rather than parroted. And our reinvention meter registered exactly as
designed: three consecutive cold agents independently rediscovered the
same four unattributable payment rows — each presenting the discovery
as new, because the capsule didn't yet carry it — and the moment a
working session finally recorded the caveat, the next agent retrieved
it perfectly. Negative knowledge transfers immediately once recorded;
the cost is every rediscovery before that.

**The honest caveats, pre-committed before any round ran:** one agent
per round, so a four-point spread may be sampling noise; the micro
questions deliberately over-represent interesting failure modes; a
growing capsule is confounded with growing derived data and time; and
this measures whether the *artifact stack* helps cold agents — it is
not, and was never, a claim about a model improving itself. One more,
recorded by the judge with a candor we've decided to institutionalize:
the blinding was partially defeated by artifact-state fingerprints
(a set that cites a caveat that didn't exist earlier dates itself), so
the procedure required the judge to declare any involuntary ordering
inference rather than pretend blindness. The declaration matched the
true order exactly — and the scores are grounded question-by-question
against the key, where anyone can re-audit them.

**What we take from it.** Not that recording knowledge is futile — the
floor results and the post-recording transfers argue the opposite.
The lesson is sharper: a capsule transmits its errors with the same
fidelity as its truths. A stale match inherited at "exact" grade is
worse than no match, because the cold consumer prices the label, not
the derivation. So the binding constraint on compounding recorded
know-how is not recall, and not volume — it is the **verification
state attached to what is recalled**: matches should carry *how* they
matched (a current name? a previous name? which vintage?), methods
should carry the scope conditions under which they're valid, and
promotion rules should demand that the corroborating artifact actually
be about the entity in question. Those are buildable requirements, not
aspirations, and they came out of a measurement designed so it was
allowed to say no to us. It did; that's the point of the seal.
